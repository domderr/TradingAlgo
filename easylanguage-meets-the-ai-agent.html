Stocks & Commodities, December 2026

EasyLanguage Meets the AI Agent

Testing intramonth seasonality beyond the backtest: what the trading platform cannot do, the agent can.

Excerpt

Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities, Volume 44, Issue 12 (December 2026), pp. 22–28, “Artificial Intelligence” section. Read the full article in the magazine.

From a statistical effect to a tradable rule

A statistical effect is not a strategy. After the agent confirmed the late-month effect out of sample (the subject of my October article), I wrote a simple rule in EasyLanguage and ran it on TradeStation: buy on a given calendar day of the month, exit after a fixed number of sessions.

On DIA daily bars, from 2010 to 2025, the result is positive. But in a rising market it could hardly be otherwise.

The missing test: does it beat buy and hold?

TradeStation and MultiCharts have no native command for one of the most useful questions about a long-only strategy: does it beat buy and hold? I asked the agent to run the same rule against it, on the same data and with the same 100 shares.

The strategy earns less in absolute terms (+293% against +355%), but it is invested only 43% of the time, with a higher Sharpe ratio (0.70 against 0.66) and a smaller drawdown (about −24% against −37%).

This is the right way to judge a long-only rule: against the benchmark, and per unit of risk and of time in the market.

Robustness, across parameters and across stocks

Neither TradeStation, MultiCharts nor TradingView offers an automatic robustness analysis. With an agent it becomes easy: a grid search on entry day and holding period, drawn as a Sharpe surface with buy and hold as the reference plane, shows where results are stable and where they are not.

Then the same rule on the 30 stocks of the Dow Jones, from 2008 to October 2026. For every one of the 30 stocks, the average strategy Sharpe ratio is below its own buy-and-hold Sharpe (a mean of 0.41 against 0.57).

A setup that holds across markets deserves much more confidence than one that works on a single symbol.

The research cycle

The workflow is a loop: the trader states an idea, the agent reads the literature and tests it out of sample, the rule is written in EasyLanguage, TradeStation executes and validates it, and the agent adds what the platform cannot do: comparison with buy and hold, parameter grids and multi-symbol scans.

The platform remains the execution and validation engine; the agent is the research layer around it. The agent is most reliable where its output can be checked mechanically, as in coding, and least reliable where the decision rests on judgment and responsibility.
