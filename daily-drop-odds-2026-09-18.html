Odds of a −2% or −3% day

The chance that at least one session of next week closes 2% or 3% below the previous close, on each of the seven Hedge Gauge markets, and what drives it.

  • 7 markets
  • 11,730 market-weeks
  • 1990–2026 history
  • Closes to 18 Sep 2026

Italy is the one market with a normal level of risk: a 26% chance of a −2% day, level with its long-run share. The other six are below their own norms, the Nasdaq 100 and the S&P 500 by about half. For a −3% day every market is below normal, between 1% (S&P 500, Australia) and 6% (Italy).

t-statistics are clustered by calendar week, because the seven markets move together. |t| above 2 is conventionally significant. Distances and ranges are computed on each market’s own index closes.

Method and limits

  • The model: a weekly logistic regression on the seven markets pooled, with a separate level for each, over 11,730 market-weeks from 1990 to 2026. The inputs are the state of the market at Friday’s close and calendar markers; the outcome is whether any close in the following Monday–Friday week was at least 2% (or 3%) below the previous close. Only inputs with a statistically visible effect are kept.
  • Out-of-sample test: fitted on data to 2012 and tested on 2013–2026, the model scores 0.364 against 0.432 for the long-run frequency at −2%, and 0.172 against 0.219 at −3% (log-loss, lower is better).
  • Cross-check: in past weeks with similar volatility and the same side of the 200-day average, a −2% day occurred in 5% of weeks for the S&P 500, 14% Nasdaq 100, 4% FTSE 100, 7% DAX, 6% Euro Stoxx 50, 16% FTSE MIB and 8% ASX 200. The model is higher for the three continental markets because they sit near the bottom of their 3-month range and fell last week.
  • Event adjustment: the figures above are the model’s multiplied by 1.15. That factor is a judgement, not an estimate, for risks history cannot measure: a Fed in a hiking cycle, the US–Iran conflict with oil near $100, and very weak consumer sentiment, partly offset by a light calendar. Before it, the −2% figures are 22.7% for Italy, 16.2% Europe50, 14.1% Nasdaq 100, 13.0% Germany, 7.7% UK, 6.5% S&P 500 and 6.1% Australia. A range of 1.0 to 1.4 times the model is reasonable.
  • Limits: 36 years and highly correlated markets carry less information than the number of weeks suggests. Indices are in local currency, not in the USD ETFs used by Hedge Gauge. Fed meeting weeks and inflation releases could not be tested, for lack of a dated series. Calendar items and pricing come from news and data-provider pages and were not checked against primary releases.
